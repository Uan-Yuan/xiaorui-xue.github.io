I am a Ph.D. student at Boston College's Lynch School of Education and Human Development, working with [Dr. Ido Davidesco](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/lynch-school/faculty-research/faculty-directory/Ido-Davidesco.html) in the [Lab-to-Classroom Research Group](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/schools/lynch-school/sites/labs/lab-to-classroom.html). Before BC, I studied cognitive psychology and educational policy at Vanderbilt University, where I worked with [Dr. Daniel Levin](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/psychological_sciences/bio/daniel-levin) on an NSF-funded study of eye tracking and learning with intelligent tutoring systems in <strong class="tag-classroom">real classrooms</strong>. I also led a child-development research team at [Parent Lab](https://myparentlab.com/).

I run mobile eye-tracking studies in the <strong class="tag-lab">lab</strong> and in <strong class="tag-classroom">real classrooms</strong>, and use PsychoPy, Python, and R to build experiments and analyze data.

<h2 id="research">My research interests</h2>

Broadly, I am interested in understanding **visual attention** and how it interacts with **ongoing thought** to shape what people **perceive**, **attend** to, and **learn** from the world around them.

<p class="muted">More specifically, I aim to bridge <strong class="tag-lab">controlled lab research</strong> and <strong class="tag-classroom">real classrooms</strong>, using mobile eye-tracking glasses 👓 to better understand what students see, think, and learn in naturalistic settings.</p>
