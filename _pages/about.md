I work with [Dr. Ido Davidesco](https://www.bc.edu/bc-web/schools/lynch-school/sites/labs/lab-to-classroom.html) in the Lab-to-Classroom Lab. <h2 id="research">My research interests</h2>

Broadly, I am interested in **visual attention** and how it interacts with **ongoing thought**. Together, they shape what people **perceive**, **attend** to, and **learn** from the world around them.

<p class="muted">More specifically, I aim to bridge <strong class="tag-lab">controlled lab research</strong> and <strong class="tag-classroom">real classrooms</strong>. I use mobile eye tracking 👓 to capture what students see, think, and learn, through first-person video and gaze data.</p>

<ol class="questions">
  <li>Can our eyes reveal what we are <strong>thinking</strong>, in a <strong class="tag-classroom">real classroom</strong>?</li>
  <li>Does attention in the <strong class="tag-lab">lab</strong> look like attention in the <strong class="tag-classroom">classroom</strong>?</li>
  <li>How does our <strong>everyday environment</strong> shape what we perceive, attend to, and learn?</li>
</ol>

## What I do now

In my current work, students wear lightweight eye-tracking glasses in their regular university lectures. During class, they answer brief thought probes and quiz questions. I align their gaze features with these moment-to-moment reports of mind wandering. The features include where they look, how long they fixate, how often they blink, and how much their heads move. The goal is to test whether eye and head movements can serve as markers of attention and learning outside the lab.

In a parallel line of work, I bring the same students into the lab for controlled attention tasks. I use their lab data to train machine-learning models. Then I test whether these models can predict the same students' attention and learning in their real classrooms. I am also involved in the eye-tracking component of our NSF-funded project on engagement in intelligent tutoring systems.

<p class="muted"><strong>Methods:</strong> mobile eye tracking · EEG · thought probes &amp; quizzes · multimodal behavioral coding · generalized linear mixed models · computer vision &amp; AI annotation · R &amp; Python</p>

## Selected research experience

### 💻 Linking Eye Movements with Visual Attention to Enhance Cyberlearning

From 2019 to 2022, I worked with [Dr. Daniel Levin](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/psychological_sciences/bio/daniel-levin) and Dr. Gautam Biswas at the Levin Lab at Vanderbilt (NSF Award 1623625). Middle school students learned with an intelligent tutoring system in their real classrooms while we tracked their eyes. We designed coding schemes that link students' eye movements to their learning. This work led to a [first-authored paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-022-10154-4) in *Educational Technology Research and Development* (2022). The paper examines the prevalence and causes of eye-tracking data loss in a middle school classroom.

### 🧒 Early childhood research: Peabody Research Office & SRI International

As a research assistant at the [Peabody Research Office](https://peabody.vanderbilt.edu/research/peabody-research-office/), I visited public pre-K classrooms across Tennessee. I observed children's interactions and daily routines. This gave me a close look at curriculum design, staffing, and the challenges these programs face.

From 2019 to 2020, I was a research assistant at [SRI International](https://www.sri.com/). I individually assessed children in Metro Nashville public pre-Ks. The assessments focused on social-emotional growth, academic skills, and language development.

Both experiences showed me the gap between students from high- and low-income communities. They pushed me to ask how learning environments shape both academic achievement and social-emotional learning. I also wrote a [policy brief]({{ base_path }}/files/policy-brief.pdf) on universal pre-K reforms in the United States.

### 🏙️ Education technology: Parent Lab

At [Parent Lab](https://myparentlab.com/), I led a research team grounded in child development theory. We designed curricula for parents and educators on parenting practices and developmental psychology. We also built parent-facing assessments and produced bite-sized, personalized parenting content for caregivers.
